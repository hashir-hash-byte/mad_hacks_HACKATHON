Build something nobody needs.

MAD HACKS is an internal college hackathon with a twist: the project has to be useless, and it still has to work. Teams of four get 24 hours to turn a bad idea into a working prototype, then demo it live in 3 minutes.

Pick one theme

Every team chooses one of three. Scroll through them and decide how you want to waste your night.

🔧

Theme 1: Useless Hardware

A gadget that solves a problem nobody has.

  • Build a machine or contraption from components, junk or old gadgets.
  • Bring your own microcontroller, sensors, motors, wires and batteries.
  • Battery or USB power only. No mains-voltage hardware.
  • Phone sensors, old gadgets and borrowed lab kits (arranged with a faculty member) are welcome.
  • Keep it safe to touch: no sharp edges, hot parts or exposed wiring. The audience will try it.
  • Soldering needs a faculty coordinator's approval.
  • Record a short video of it working, in case it fails during the demo.
⚙️

Theme 2: Over-Engineered Solutions

Take a simple everyday task and solve it in the most complicated way possible.

  • The solution must still finish the task from start to end.
  • Show the complexity in your demo: a diagram, or a count of the steps involved.
  • Software, hardware or a mix of both. Free tools only.
💻

Theme 3: Useless Software

Websites, apps, bots, games or AI projects that are delightfully pointless.

  • Annoying or pointless is fine. Harmful is not: no malicious code, no collecting real personal data, nothing that locks up a device.
  • No rapidly flashing lights or very loud sounds.
  • Keep a local copy that runs without the internet.
  • Using AI? Stick to free tiers or models on your own laptop, keep API keys private, don't imitate real people, and check the output for offensive content before the demo.
  • Test the demo on the machine you'll present from, and tell the audience which tools and models you used.

Rules

Teams and build

  • Exactly 4 members per team.
  • Every team must build a prototype.
  • All work starts after the opening session. Pre-built projects are not eligible.
  • Any tools are allowed, AI assistants included, but be honest about what you used.

Progress reports

  • Ideas must be ready by 1:00 pm.
  • At 1:00 pm the judges ask what your idea is. You're scored on how fluently you answer, so every member should be able to speak.
  • At midnight, explain the changes you made from their input.
  • You can ask the judges for mentorship at any time.

Safety and conduct

  • Projects must be safe and non-offensive.
  • No mains-voltage hardware. No jokes aimed at individuals.
  • Be respectful to every team, judge and volunteer. Keep noise down at night.
  • Report injuries, damaged equipment or unsafe wiring to the organizers right away.

Demo and scoring

  • Every team gives a 3-minute live demo, and the audience tries it where possible.
  • Say what it does, why it's useless, and what you built during the event.
  • Final score = judges' evaluation + the audience's average rating.
  • The top 3 teams get prizes.

What to bring

The organizers provide no equipment, and meals are not provided.

Every team

  • Laptops and chargers for everyone who will code
  • Extension boards. Power points are limited.
  • Phones and chargers (sensors, camera, hotspot)
  • College ID cards
  • Water bottle, light jacket, personal items for the night
  • Your own food, or plan to order in

Hardware teams

  • Microcontrollers, sensors, motors, buzzers, wires, batteries
  • Screwdriver, tape, wire cutters
  • Old gadgets, spare parts, cardboard
  • Lab kits, if arranged with a faculty member beforehand

Stay sane

  • Install tools and offline docs before the event.
  • Back up often with Git or a cloud drive.
  • Keep a mobile hotspot ready.
  • Rest in turns, but keep at least one member with the project.
  • Tell a volunteer before leaving the venue at night. Feeling unwell? Tell an organizer immediately.
